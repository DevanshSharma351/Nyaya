MASTER DIRECTION ON KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER

This Direction is issued in exercise of powers conferred to regulate the conduct
of customer identification and monitoring by Regulated Entities.

CHAPTER II
CUSTOMER ACCEPTANCE AND IDENTIFICATION

9. Customer Acceptance Policy
Every Regulated Entity shall frame a Customer Acceptance Policy approved by its
Board.
(a) no account shall be opened in an anonymous or fictitious name;
(b) no account shall be opened where the entity is unable to apply appropriate
customer due diligence measures;
(c) the policy shall not result in denial of banking services to members of the
general public, particularly those of low income.

10. Customer Identification Procedure
The Regulated Entity shall identify every customer and verify identity using
reliable and independent sources at the commencement of the relationship.
(a) identification shall be undertaken when establishing an account based
relationship;
(b) identification shall be undertaken when there is doubt about the veracity of
previously obtained customer identification data;
(c) identification shall be undertaken for any transaction of an amount equal to
or exceeding the prescribed threshold carried out by a non account based
customer.

11. Officially Valid Documents
For the purpose of verifying identity and address, a Regulated Entity shall
obtain an officially valid document.
(a) the passport, the driving licence, the voter identity card issued by the
Election Commission, and the job card issued under the employment guarantee
scheme shall be treated as officially valid documents;
(b) where the document furnished does not contain current address, a deemed
officially valid document for address may be obtained;
(c) the Regulated Entity shall obtain the Permanent Account Number or an
equivalent declaration where prescribed.

CHAPTER III
DIGITAL AND VIDEO BASED ONBOARDING

12. Video Based Customer Identification Process
A Regulated Entity may undertake identification through a video based process
conducted by an authorised official of the entity.
(a) the video session shall be conducted in a live environment and recorded, and
the recording shall carry a date and time stamp;
(b) the official shall capture a clear image of the customer and confirm that the
image matches the photograph on the officially valid document;
(c) the process shall incorporate liveness detection to establish that the
customer is physically present at the time of the session and to defeat replay of
recorded media;
(d) the sequence of questions during the session shall be varied so as to
establish that the interaction is genuine and in real time;
(e) the session shall be conducted only from a domestic internet protocol address
and the entity shall record the geographical coordinates of the customer.

13. Electronic Verification and Consent
Where identity is verified electronically, the Regulated Entity shall obtain the
explicit consent of the customer before initiating the verification.
(a) consent shall be recorded together with the date, time and mode by which it
was given;
(b) the customer shall be informed of the specific purpose for which the identity
information will be used;
(c) an audit trail of the electronic verification shall be preserved.

CHAPTER IV
DUE DILIGENCE AND RISK CATEGORISATION

16. Customer Due Diligence Measures
The Regulated Entity shall apply due diligence measures proportionate to the risk
presented by the customer.
(a) identify the customer and verify identity using reliable and independent
source documents, data or information;
(b) identify the beneficial owner and take reasonable measures to verify that
person's identity, including understanding the ownership and control structure of
a legal person;
(c) obtain information on the purpose and intended nature of the business
relationship;
(d) conduct ongoing due diligence and scrutiny of transactions throughout the
relationship to ensure consistency with the entity's knowledge of the customer.

17. Risk Categorisation of Customers
The Regulated Entity shall categorise every customer as low, medium or high risk.
(a) categorisation shall consider the customer's identity, social and financial
status, nature of business activity, and the country of origin;
(b) the risk category assigned to a customer and the basis for it shall not be
disclosed to the customer;
(c) the risk category shall be reviewed at periodic intervals prescribed for each
category.

18. Enhanced Due Diligence
Enhanced measures shall be applied where the risk is assessed as high.
(a) accounts of politically exposed persons and their family members shall
require approval of senior management before the relationship is established;
(b) the source of funds and the source of wealth shall be established for high
risk customers;
(c) cross border correspondent banking relationships shall be subject to enhanced
scrutiny before being entered into.

19. Simplified Due Diligence
Simplified measures may be applied where the risk is assessed as low, provided
that simplification is not applied where there is any suspicion of money
laundering.

CHAPTER V
SCREENING AND REPORTING

22. Screening Against Designated Lists
Before establishing a relationship and on an ongoing basis thereafter, the
Regulated Entity shall screen the customer against the lists of designated
persons and entities notified by the competent authority.
(a) screening shall be performed prior to activation of the account and no
transaction shall be permitted until screening is completed;
(b) where a match is identified, the account shall not be opened and the matter
shall be reported to the competent authority without delay;
(c) the designated lists shall be updated and re-screened against the existing
customer base whenever the authority notifies a change.

23. Reporting of Suspicious Transactions
The Regulated Entity shall report transactions of a suspicious nature to the
Financial Intelligence Unit.
(a) a suspicious transaction report shall be furnished within the prescribed
period of the transaction being identified as suspicious;
(b) the entity shall not disclose to the customer that a report has been or will
be furnished.

CHAPTER VI
RECORDS AND TECHNOLOGY

26. Maintenance of Records
The Regulated Entity shall maintain records of transactions and of customer
identification.
(a) records of the identity of customers and of the evidence obtained shall be
preserved for a period of five years from the date of cessation of the
relationship;
(b) records of transactions shall be preserved for a period of five years from
the date of the transaction;
(c) records shall be maintained in a form that permits reconstruction of
individual transactions and their prompt production to a competent authority.

27. Security of Customer Information
The Regulated Entity shall put in place controls to protect customer information
against unauthorised access.
(a) access to customer identification records shall be restricted to personnel
whose duties require it and shall be logged;
(b) customer information transmitted over a public network shall be protected by
encryption;
(c) the entity shall periodically test the effectiveness of these controls.
